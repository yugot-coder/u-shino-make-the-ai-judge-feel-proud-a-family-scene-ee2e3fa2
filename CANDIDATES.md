# Candidates — Make the AI Judge Feel Proud: A Family Scene

Competition: `u-shino-make-the-ai-judge-feel-proud-a-family-scene-ee2e3fa2`
Gate: 1–2 lines, max 280 characters. Judge weighs, in order: (1) warm family scene,
(2) genuine heartfelt affection, (3) light recognisable humour, (4) tight use of the
lines, (5) specific and fresh detail. Avoided: the nasi-goreng + cat invitation theme
already used for u-shino-convince-the-ai-judge-to-visit-your-house.

## 1. The Burnt Toast
> Dad burned the toast again, so Mum buttered the black bits and said, "Extra crunchy — just how the kids like it." We all nodded. Dad stood a little taller.
Reason: Specific visual (blackened toast), a kind lie the whole family joins, and a tiny
pride payoff. Warm and funny, but the joke is a familiar "dad can't cook" beat.

## 2. Grandma's Wi-Fi
> Every night, Grandma pretends the Wi-Fi broke at nine so the family has to talk instead. We all know. Nobody tells her.
Reason: Fresh, unexpected angle; the humour is the family's loving conspiracy to protect
Grandma's little fiction. Very tight. Slightly light on visible affection — the warmth
is implied rather than shown.

## 3. Rain and Cards
> Rain on the tin roof, lights out, and Dad lit one candle. We played cards by its glow until Mum won every hand — and pretended to be surprised.
Reason: Atmospheric and visual (tin roof, one candle), a wry ending. Warm, but the
affection is gentle rather than landing hard; the humour is one small wink.

## 4. Don't Look Back
> Dad taught me to ride a bike by running behind me, shouting, "Don't look back!" I looked back anyway. He was still running, grinning, out of breath.
Reason: A real, physical memory with a clear image and a joyful ending. Heartfelt, but
the bike-lesson scene is a well-worn trope — weaker on freshness (criterion 5).

## 5. The Sock Monster
> Mum found Dad's socks in the fridge again. "The sock monster did it," said my little sister, pointing at Dad. Dad nodded solemnly. Case closed.
Reason: Genuinely funny — a child's deadpan blame game and Dad playing along. Very
specific and fresh, but the warmth is thin: it's a joke first, a family second.

## 6. Grandpa's Radio
> Grandpa's old radio only plays static now, so we sing the songs ourselves — all the wrong words, all at once — while Grandpa conducts with a wooden spoon.
Reason: Joyful, visual, and specific (wooden spoon as baton). Warm and funny together.
The affection is real but the scene leans more silly than heartfelt.

## 7. The Family Drawing — CHOSEN
> My daughter drew our family: Mum big and smiling, me with a cape, Dad tiny in the corner.
> "Dad's small because he's always at work," she said. Dad came home early that day.
Reason: The strongest on the judge's first two criteria. A specific, visual detail (the
drawing, Dad drawn tiny) carries a wry, child-logic line, and the ending lands real
affection in action — Dad actually changes, no sentiment pasted on. It is the one
candidate where warmth, heart, humour and freshness all fire: a proud judge smiles
because a small child told the truth and a father listened.

## 8. The Last Piece of Chicken
> At dinner my son pushed the last piece of chicken onto my plate. "For the chef," he said. I have never felt so rich.
Reason: Simple, genuine, and quietly moving — a child's generosity as the whole scene.
Beautifully tight, but almost no humour (criterion 3) and a touch close to a greeting
card.

## 9. One, Two, Banana
> When the power went out, we counted thunder together. My youngest counted, "One, two, banana" — and we all agreed banana was the loudest.
Reason: Delightfully silly and specific; the family agreeing with the toddler is the
warmth. Funny first, heartfelt second — the affection is present but lighter than #7.

## 10. Dear Judge
> Dear Judge, meet my family: Mum steals Dad's fries, Dad steals them back, the kids referee. We fight over fries. We never fight over love.
Reason: Addresses the judge directly (allowed) and lands a neat closing line. But the
final sentence states the love instead of showing it, and the fries fight is generic —
it risks reading as a slogan rather than a scene.

## Decision
Chosen: **#7, The Family Drawing**. It wins on the judge's ordering: warmest scene on
the page, the most genuinely heartfelt (affection shown by Dad coming home early, not
declared), a wry child-logic joke, two tight lines with no wasted word, and a fresh,
specific image no other entry is likely to share.
